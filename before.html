Origins

The first FIFA World Cup was held in 1930 in Uruguay, chosen partly because the country had won Olympic football gold in 1924 and 1928 and was celebrating its centennial of independence that year. Only 13 teams took part, mostly from South America and Europe, since a long boat trip discouraged many European sides from entering. Uruguay hosted and won the tournament, beating Argentina 4-2 in the final in front of a crowd in Montevideo. The tournament has been held every four years since, with two exceptions: 1942 and 1946, when it was cancelled because of the Second World War. The competition has grown enormously since then, from 13 teams in 1930 to 48 teams starting in 2026, and it is now the most watched sporting event in the world by a wide margin.

Records

Brazil has won the most titles with five, in 1958, 1962, 1970, 1994, and 2002, and is the only team to have played in every World Cup. Germany and Italy are next with four titles each. Argentina has three titles, most recently in 2022. Uruguay, France, and Spain have each won twice, and England has won once, in 1966, as host. Brazil's Pele is the only player to win the tournament three times, in 1958, 1962, and 1970.

The 2026 Tournament

The 2026 World Cup was the 23rd edition of the tournament and the first to be co-hosted by three countries: the United States, Canada, and Mexico. It was also the first to feature 48 teams, up from 32, making it the largest World Cup ever held. Spain won the tournament, beating three-time champions Argentina 1-0 in extra time in the final at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey, with the winning goal scored by Ferran Torres. It was Spain's second World Cup title, after their first in 2010.
